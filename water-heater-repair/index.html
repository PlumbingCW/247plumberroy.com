Urgent first

No hot water or a leaking tank needs attention now

A water heater that leaks is more urgent than one that is cold. If water is pooling around the base, turn off the cold supply valve above the tank, switch the gas valve to pilot or the breaker off, and call. Slow weeps from a fitting can sometimes be repaired, but water from the tank body means the tank has failed.

If the heater simply stopped making hot water, you have a little more time. Check that the breaker has not tripped or the pilot has not gone out, and call us if it persists. Our 24/7 line takes both kinds of call.

Repair or replace

How to decide whether the tank is worth fixing

Age and condition matter more than the single part that failed.

Repair makes sense when

  • The tank is not leaking from the shell.
  • The failed part is a thermostat, element, valve or pilot assembly.
  • The heater is within the middle of its expected service life.
  • Everything else on the unit is in good shape.

Replace makes sense when

  • Water is escaping from the tank body.
  • Heavy rust shows at the base or fittings.
  • The heater is near the end of its life and has had other repairs.
  • You want to change size or type, as with a tankless unit.

The nameplate on the side of the tank shows the manufacture date, usually in a serial number code. Have it ready when you call.

Local conditions

Hard water and pressure in Roy water heaters

Roy blends water from its own wells with treated water from the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District. Weber Basin reports hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for its supply, which is hard water. Scale from hard water settles on elements and the tank bottom, so heaters can run noisy and slow to recover as they age.

Roy's water plan also notes delivery pressure between 60 and 90 psi. High pressure puts extra strain on tank seams, relief valves and fittings. A pressure gauge on an outdoor spigot tells you where you stand, and a plumber can fit a regulator or an expansion tank if the number is high.

Safety

Safety checklist: what to do at the heater

A few quick checks prevent the problems that turn a repair into an emergency.

  • Look for rust or water at the base and fittings.
  • Check that the temperature and pressure relief valve has a discharge pipe pointed toward the floor.
  • Keep storage and flammables away from gas heaters.
  • Confirm exhaust venting is connected on gas units.
  • Do not tamper with a relief valve that drips, since it signals a pressure or temperature issue worth diagnosing.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us.

What not to do

  • Do not remove or plug the temperature and pressure relief valve.
  • Do not relight a gas pilot if you smell gas.
  • Do not drain a hot tank into a closed space.
  • Do not tamper with the burner or vent on a gas unit.
  • Call a plumber if the same breaker or pilot problem repeats.
