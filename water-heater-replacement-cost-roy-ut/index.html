Honest framing

Water heater replacement prices depend on the install, not just the tank

The tag price of a water heater is only part of the bill. The size, fuel type, venting, permits, code upgrades and the condition of the old connections all change the total. That is why two quotes for the same tank can differ and why a quote without a site visit is only a rough guess.

This guide explains the factors so you can compare quotes. If a tank is leaking now, shut off the supply and call. For the service, see water heater replacement and planned installation.

Extras

Options worth asking about

A few extras are worth a conversation. An expansion tank protects the system in a closed plumbing setup. A drain pan with a line catches a small leak before it reaches the floor. A leak sensor gives an early alert. A pressure reducing valve protects the heater and other fixtures if your pressure is high.

Ask the plumber which of these are required and which are optional, and why. A plain answer is a good sign. See tankless water heaters if you are weighing a different type.

Extra

Warranty terms worth reading

Water heater warranties usually come in two parts. The manufacturer covers the tank, and sometimes parts, for a stated number of years. The installer covers labor for a shorter period. The two are not the same, and many homeowners assume the labor is covered for as long as the tank. Ask for both in writing.

Read what the tank warranty covers. Many cover the tank only against leaks from defects, not against sediment or scale damage, and some require registration or yearly maintenance to stay valid. Others reduce the benefit after the first few years, so the replacement unit is discounted rather than free.

A higher priced unit with a longer warranty is not always the better deal. It depends on how long you plan to stay in the house and how hard your water is. A plumber who explains the real terms, instead of repeating the marketing number, is easier to trust.

Before help arrives

What to do if the tank is leaking while you wait for a quote

A leaking tank is a safety and damage issue.

  • Close the cold water supply valve above the tank.
  • Turn a gas unit to pilot or off, or switch an electric unit off at the breaker.
  • Do not touch the tank or the discharge pipe, since the water can be very hot.
  • Place towels or a pan to limit floor damage.
  • Call and describe the leak so a plumber can prioritize it.
Extra

Questions to ask when you call

A short list of questions helps you compare quotes. Ask what size and type is being quoted and why. Ask whether venting, a relief valve, a discharge line and an expansion tank are included. Ask how the old tank is handled and whether a permit is part of the job.

Then ask when the work could happen and how long the house will be without hot water. A plumber who answers in plain terms, without pressure, is usually one who will be straightforward on the day.

Roy, UT local note: pressure and hard water

Roy City's 2024 water conservation plan describes delivery pressure of 60 to 90 psi. Pressure near the top of that range is a reason to ask about a pressure reducing valve and an expansion tank, because high pressure shortens water heater life. A gauge on an outdoor spigot gives you your own reading.

Weber Basin's water quality report lists hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for its supply, which Roy blends with city wells. Hard water leaves scale that shortens heater life, so ask about the anode rod and about flushing. Neighbors in Clearfield and Layton are on the same Weber Basin supply.
