Emergency

Emergency plumber in Roy, available 24/7

A plumbing emergency does not wait for business hours. A supply line can let go at 3 a.m., a main line can back up on a Sunday, and a water heater can start leaking on a holiday. Our dispatch line is staffed 24/7 so you reach a person who can start sending help, not a voicemail box.

When you call, tell us what you see, where the water is, and whether you can reach the main shutoff. We use that to decide whether the problem is an emergency plumber call tonight or a same-day appointment when available. Either way you get a clear price before work starts.

Before we arrive

What to do in the first ten minutes of a plumbing emergency

These four steps limit damage while an emergency plumber is on the way. They are safe for most homeowners and do not need tools.

1

Shut off the main water.

Close the main shutoff valve, usually where the line enters the house near the water heater or in the garage or basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2

Stop using drains.

For a backup, stop running sinks, showers, dishwashers and washing machines so more water does not push into the line.

3

Keep power away from water.

If water is near outlets, appliances or a panel, stay out and turn the breaker off only if you can reach it dry and safely.

4

Call the 24/7 line.

Call (385) 488-1892, describe what you see, and move valuables out of the wet area while you wait.

A sixth step costs nothing: take a few photos of the water and the damaged area before cleanup, since your insurance company may ask for them. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Water in Roy

How the water system around Roy affects your plumbing

Roy City runs its own culinary water system. According to the city's 2024 water conservation plan it draws on four city wells and buys additional treated water from the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, serving about 40,000 people across roughly seven square miles.

Delivery pressure at homes is generally between 60 and 90 psi, which is on the high side. High pressure is hard on water heaters, toilet fill valves and supply lines, and a plumber can test it with a gauge on an outdoor spigot.

The same plan explains that new city water lines are mostly PVC because of corrosive soil concerns. That is a clue for homeowners with older buried metal lines on their own property, since the soil that worries the city can also wear on private pipe. Outdoor irrigation water in Roy comes from a separate system run by the Roy Water Conservancy District, so a soggy yard does not always mean a leak in your drinking water line.

Weber Basin's published water quality report lists hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for the water it supplies. Roy blends that supply with its own wells, so the number at your tap depends on the mix. Hard water leaves scale in water heaters and tankless units, which is why we talk about flushing and softener sizing on those jobs.

Local checklist

What to have ready when you call about a Roy plumbing problem

A good first call takes two minutes when you have a few details ready.

  • What you see or hear, such as water on the floor, a gurgling drain or no hot water.
  • Where it is in the house and whether the water is still running.
  • Whether you could reach and close the main shutoff valve.
  • The age of the water heater if the problem involves hot water, which is printed on its label.
  • The nearest cross street, such as 1900 West or 5600 South, plus your ZIP code.

None of this is required. If you do not know an answer, we will walk you through finding it.
