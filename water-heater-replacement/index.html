Urgent first

A leaking tank is an emergency replacement call

When a tank leaks from the shell, repair is not possible. Water is escaping through a rusted seam or a failed liner, and the leak only grows. Shut off the cold supply valve above the tank, turn the gas valve to pilot or the breaker off, and drain nothing until a plumber arrives unless water is running toward finished floors.

We take these calls 24/7. Tell us the tank size from the label if you can read it, and whether the unit is gas or electric. That lets the plumber bring the right replacement on the first trip when stock allows.

Process

From the call to hot water again

Replacement is one of the more predictable jobs in plumbing, which is why the price can be clear before work starts.

  1. 1

    Confirm the unit and location.

    Fuel type, tank size, venting and where it sits all affect which water heater fits and what the install needs.

  2. 2

    Present options and price.

    You see a clear price for the size that fits your household, and the plumber explains the difference between standard and higher-efficiency units.

  3. 3

    Drain, remove and install.

    The old tank is drained and removed. The new one is set, connected, vented or wired to code and fitted with a new relief valve and discharge line.

  4. 4

    Test and walk through.

    The plumber checks for leaks, exhaust draw on gas units and temperature, then shows you the shutoff and the settings.

Sizing

How household size relates to tank size

Use this as a general guide. The plumber looks at your actual hot water habits before recommending a size.

HouseholdTank size often usedNotes
One to two people.30 to 40 gallons.Short showers and small laundry loads.
Three to four people.40 to 50 gallons.The most common size in single-family homes.
Five or more people.50 to 75 gallons.Check the recovery rate as well as capacity.
Large tubs or heavy use.Higher capacity or tankless.Peak demand matters more than daily use.

An oversized tank costs more to heat and an undersized one runs out. Getting the size right is a design choice, not a guess.

Tank or tankless

The two replacement paths

Most homes in Roy still use storage tanks, but tankless is a real option when the gas supply and venting allow it.

Standard tank

  • Lower upfront cost and simple installation.
  • Keeps a reservoir of hot water ready.
  • Standby heat loss runs all day.
  • Typical for replacement in the same location.

Tankless

  • Heats water on demand, so supply does not run out.
  • Needs adequate gas line and venting, sometimes upgrades.
  • Scale in hard water calls for periodic flushing.
  • Details on the tankless page.
Local conditions

What Roy water does to a new water heater

Roy City draws on its own wells and buys treated water from the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, and the city's 2024 conservation plan describes delivery pressure between 60 and 90 psi. High pressure is a common reason relief valves weep and tanks age early. An expansion tank is often part of a good installation in a closed system.

Weber Basin's water quality report lists hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon. That is hard water, and it leaves scale on elements and tank bottoms. Ask about the anode rod and about flushing intervals, and consider softener sizing if scale is a recurring complaint.

Before the install

How to prepare for replacement day

A little preparation makes the visit faster.

  • Clear a path to the water heater and the area around it.
  • Know where the water shutoff and the breaker or gas valve are.
  • Move stored items away from the tank.
  • Tell the plumber about any past issues with venting, noise or drains.
  • Plan to be home, since a code-compliant install includes a walkthrough.

Before help arrives

What to do before help arrives when a tank is leaking

Hot water and electricity or gas call for care.

  • Close the cold supply valve above the tank.
  • Turn a gas unit to pilot or off, or switch an electric unit off at the breaker.
  • Do not touch the tank or the discharge pipe, since the water can be very hot.
  • Keep children and pets away from the area.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first.
