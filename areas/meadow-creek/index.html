Meadow Creek

Plumbing service for Meadow Creek homeowners in northwest Roy

The Roy City 2023 General Plan lists Meadow Creek as a northwest Roy neighborhood of single-family homes from the 1990s to the present. It names Foxglen Park as one of two parks, and it notes the Roy segments of the Layton Canal and Howard Slough.

The plan also mentions Meadow Creek Pond as a local fishing spot, and two commercial nodes along 3500 West. It calls the neighborhood park poor and recommends new parks and canal trails.

Newer homes

Plumbing questions in homes built in the last 30 years

A newer house is not free of plumbing needs, but the questions differ. Instead of old pipe, owners ask about water heaters reaching their first replacement, fixtures wearing out and water quality.

Weber Basin lists hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for the water it supplies, and Roy blends that with its own wells. Scale builds up faster in hard water, so ask whether a softener or filter suits your house.

Practical signs to watch for are white crust on faucets, spotted dishes and a water heater that rumbles. Draining a few gallons from the heater tank once a year, as its manual describes, removes sediment and shows how much scale the water leaves.

Water around the house

Sorting out where basement water comes from

Water in a basement can come from a failed pipe, a failed sump pump, poor drainage around the house or groundwater. Each has a different fix, which is why the first step is finding the source.

Our sump pump page explains how pumps work, and the basement waterproofing page covers drains and sealing. A leak check can rule a pipe in or out.

Before you call

What to jot down before calling from northwest Roy

  • The cross street, or a landmark such as Foxglen Park or 3500 West.
  • Where the water appears and when, such as after rain or all the time.
  • Whether the house has a sump pump and whether you hear it run.
  • The age of the water heater and any scale on fixtures.
  • Photos of stains, standing water or the sump pit.
Safety

First steps if water enters the basement

If water is coming in, stay away from the area if outlets or appliances are wet and turn the breaker off only if you can reach it dry. Never wade through standing water near live power.

Once it is safe, move belongings up, photograph the water and its source, and note whether a sump pump is running.
