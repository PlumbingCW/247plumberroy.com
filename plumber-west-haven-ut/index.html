Local water

Roy City helps supply water to West Haven

Roy City's own water plan notes that it sells water to the West Haven special service district. That makes West Haven's water chemistry and pressure closely tied to the Roy system, which draws on four city wells and on Weber Basin supply. Weber Basin lists hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for its water, and Roy blends sources, so ask the city for its current water quality report.

Hard water leaves scale in water heaters and on fixtures. In newer West Haven homes with tankless heaters, that scale builds in the heat exchanger. See tankless water heaters for flushing and softeners for treatment.

Before we arrive

What to do while an emergency plumber heads to West Haven

Lots here are often long, so lines run further and water can travel before you notice.

1

Close the house main.

Look near the water heater, in the garage or at the front foundation wall. A quarter of the way open is still open, so turn until firm.

2

Check the yard side.

If a spigot or yard line is spraying, the main at the meter box may be the faster shutoff.

3

Keep the breaker panel dry.

Do not stand in water to reach it. If it is wet, stay out and call 911 for any shock risk.

4

Describe the road.

Give the street name and the nearest main road, since many subdivision streets look alike.

If the water heater is involved, close its cold supply valve too. It limits the flow from the tank.

Where the rivers meet

The Weber and Ogden rivers meet here

West Haven sits near the confluence of the Weber and Ogden rivers, with the Great Salt Lake basin to the west. The ground is flat and the water table can be high, especially in spring. That makes basements and crawlspaces worth watching for seepage and makes a working sump pump valuable where one is installed.

If your pump fails during runoff, treat it as an emergency and call the 24/7 line. For seepage without a pit, see basement waterproofing. Frozen outdoor lines are the other seasonal issue, so disconnect hoses in the fall.
