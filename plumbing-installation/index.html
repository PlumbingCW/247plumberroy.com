Planned work

New fixtures and lines, installed to plan

Plumbing installation covers everything added or upgraded in a house that already works. Examples include a new bathroom, a kitchen remodel, a laundry hookup, an outdoor spigot or a water softener loop. The common thread is planning. Done right, it does not leak, it meets code and it is easy to service.

If something is failing, see our emergency repair pages. This page is about the planned side of plumbing, where you choose the day and the details.

Local conditions

Remodeling older Roy homes

Roy has a lot of homes from the 1940s through the 1980s, and remodels often uncover plumbing that was done to older codes. Galvanized lines in a wall that is being opened, cast iron drains that need to tie into new PVC, and old shutoffs that no longer seal are all worth addressing while the wall is open. See repiping if a bigger replacement is warranted.

Roy City's permits are available online, and most plumbing additions need one. The plumber knows what applies and can often coordinate inspection scheduling. The state's license lookup is a good check on any contractor you hire for plumbing work.

Before you buy

Questions to settle before ordering fixtures

A few decisions are easier before the fixtures arrive.

  • Where the water heater and shutoffs are, and whether they are accessible.
  • Whether the existing drain size supports the new fixtures.
  • The rough-in dimensions of the fixtures you chose.
  • Whether the finish faucet comes with the correct connectors.
  • Whether permits are needed and who pulls them.

Planning ahead

Common mistakes to avoid on a remodel

The most costly plumbing mistakes happen before the plumber arrives: fixtures ordered without checking rough-in dimensions, a wall closed before the rough-in was inspected, or a shower valve placed where it cannot be serviced. Each is cheap to prevent and expensive to fix.

Bring the plumber into the project early, even if only for a short walk-through. A good plumber can flag a drain that is too small or a vent that has nowhere to go while you can still change the plan on paper.
