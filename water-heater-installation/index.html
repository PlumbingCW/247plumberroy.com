Planned work

Replace a water heater before it fails

A planned installation is the better way to replace a water heater. You choose the day, you choose the unit, and nothing is flooding. Most tank heaters last about ten to fifteen years, and the age on the label tells you where yours stands. If the heater is approaching that range, scheduling replacement now avoids a failure at a bad moment.

This page is about planned installs. If your water heater is leaking now, see emergency water heater replacement, which our 24/7 line handles.

Process

What a planned water heater installation involves

Planned work lets the details be right.

  1. 1

    Choose the unit.

    Fuel type, size, efficiency and location are decided together. The plumber explains the tradeoffs without pushing a model.

  2. 2

    Review the site.

    Venting, gas line size, power, drainage and clearances are checked against code.

  3. 3

    Install and connect.

    The new unit is set, connected to water, gas or power and vented, with a relief valve and discharge pipe.

  4. 4

    Test and hand over.

    The plumber checks combustion, temperature and leaks, and shows you the controls.

Why planned beats emergency

Local conditions

What Roy water means for a new installation

Roy City's 2024 water plan reports delivery pressure of 60 to 90 psi. High pressure shortens water heater life and makes expansion tanks and pressure reducing valves a worthwhile part of installation, especially in a closed system with a check valve at the meter.

Weber Basin reports hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for its supply, and Roy blends that with its own wells. Hard water leaves scale in any heater. Ask the plumber about an anode rod, periodic flushing and, if scale is a problem in the house, a softener.

Prepare

What to do before installation day

Preparation saves time and prevents surprises.

  • Read the nameplate on the old unit to note the size and age.
  • Clear the area and the path to it.
  • Decide whether you want to change fuel type or size.
  • Ask about permits and inspection for your address.
  • Plan to be home for the walkthrough.

After installation

Keeping the new heater in good shape

A new water heater benefits from small habits. Set the temperature around 120 degrees Fahrenheit unless the plumber advises otherwise, since higher settings raise scald risk and energy use. Check the base for moisture once a month, and know where the shutoffs are.

Flushing the tank once a year helps in hard water areas, and the anode rod should be inspected periodically. These small steps are the difference between a heater that lasts and one that fails early.
