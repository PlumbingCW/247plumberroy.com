Before we arrive

What to do while an emergency plumber heads to Ogden

Older buildings share walls and lines, so a leak in one unit can reach another.

1

Shut off your unit or the building main.

Know which valve serves you. In an older building, ask the manager where the main is before a leak starts.

2

Warn the neighbors below.

If water is coming through a ceiling or wall, tell the people next door or downstairs right away.

3

Protect the floors and the wiring.

Lift rugs and electronics, and keep clear of light fixtures filling with water.

4

Call and name the building type.

Say whether it is a house, a duplex or an apartment, since shared lines change who can approve work.

If the building has a landlord or manager, tell them right away. Shared lines are often their responsibility.

Local water

Ogden uses wells, mountain streams and Weber Basin water

Ogden's water comes from several sources: city wells, mountain sources such as the Pineview and Wheeler Creek systems, and treated water from the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District. That variety means water quality and pressure change from one part of town to another, with the east bench typically at higher elevation and different pressure than the lower valley.

Weber Basin lists hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for its water. Older Ogden homes often have galvanized or mixed metals, and hard water speeds scale in them. See water heater repair and leak detection for the most common follow-ups.
