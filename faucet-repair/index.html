Types

Four faucet designs and how each one fails

Knowing the design helps explain why a drip appears.

Compression faucets.

Two handles with rubber washers that wear and let water seep past the seat. The fix is usually a new washer and seat.

Cartridge faucets.

A cartridge controls flow and temperature. Worn seals cause drips and temperature swings. Replacing the cartridge restores the faucet.

Ball and disc faucets.

Common in kitchens. Springs, seats and seals wear and leak at the base. Rebuild kits cover most of these repairs.

Pull-down and touchless faucets.

Hoses, connectors and sensors fail more often than the valve. The plumber tests each part.

Low flow on only one faucet is usually local. Low flow across the house points to pressure or main supply issues, which is a different diagnosis.

Local conditions

Why Roy faucets clog and wear

Weber Basin reports hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon for the water it supplies, and Roy blends that water with its own wells. Hard water leaves white scale on aerators and inside cartridges. That scale is the usual reason for reduced flow and for seals that wear faster than expected.

The city's water plan describes delivery pressure of 60 to 90 psi. Faucets are built for that range, but the upper end shortens the life of seals and supply lines. If a faucet fails sooner than you would expect, ask the plumber to check pressure along with the repair.

Care tips

Simple faucet habits

A faucet lasts longer when you treat it gently.

  • Avoid forcing a handle past its stop.
  • Unscrew the aerator and soak it in vinegar every few months.
  • Replace braided supply lines every several years.
  • Know where the shutoff under the sink is and test it twice a year.
  • Replace a leaky faucet before the seat is damaged.

Before help arrives

What to do before help arrives when a faucet fails

A failed faucet is usually a water problem first.

  • Close the small shutoff valve under the sink, turning it clockwise.
  • If that valve will not turn, close the main shutoff and do not force it.
  • Keep towels or a bucket under any spray or drip.
  • Move cleaning products and anything that can be damaged out of the cabinet.
  • Do not use the sink until the plumber has looked at the leak.
